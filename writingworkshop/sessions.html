What We'll Explore Together

Workshop Sessions

Each session is led by a feature writer from Mountain Home Magazine. Topics move from the basics of beginning — picking up the pen — to journaling, letter writing, personal narrative, and short stories drawn from real life.

01

Where to Begin

The hardest part of writing is starting. This session offers practical ways to begin when the blank page feels overwhelming. Feel the paper. Sharpen your pencil. Find your favorite pen. Write the alphabet in capitals — do you remember how to write a capital Q? Do you prefer printing or cursive? Write today's date and the names of the people around you. Write something. Just write.

Participants discover how to build momentum from simple observations and small acts of attention.

02

Journaling as Practice

A journal is not a diary. It is a tool for seeing more clearly. This session explores journaling as a daily or occasional habit that sharpens attention, deepens reflection, and lays the groundwork for richer writing of every kind.

Write to yourself and tell a funny story that happened this week. A journal entry doesn't have to be important — it just has to be honest.

03

Short Stories from Real Life

The best short stories come from small moments: a place, a person, a fragment of memory. This session teaches participants how to recognize story material in everyday experience and how to transform it into prose worth reading.

Write a short story about the last time you had real winter weather. Describe the first plants that appear in your yard and when they first show up. Let the specific detail carry the meaning.

04

Share a Childhood Story

Tell a child or grandchild a memorable story of your childhood. Take them back to a place beyond their imagination — your first television, telephone party lines, the one-cent candy store, bank books, learning to fly a kite, new shoes, or a new bicycle.

Hand-write a letter so vivid it demands a return letter or an unexpected phone call. Generations pass — but stories stay.

Lecture & Discussion Topics

Writing as a Human Act

Beyond the sessions, the workshop includes open discussion on what it means to write by hand, why personal stories matter, and how writing connects generations.

"Writing in cursive takes practice. Computers allow us to write in any font we want. Grab some paper and a nice pen. What a joy it is to receive a hand-written letter from family or a greeting card from a friend." — Is Letter Writing a Lost Art?
"Many of us have shared our lives with our Grandparents and then our Grandchildren — a stretch of five generations. What a gift it is to tell our Grandchildren stories of their Great Great Grandparents." — Sharing Family Legacies

What to Bring

  • A notebook or blank paper (lined or unlined)
  • Your favorite pen or pencil
  • An open mind and a willingness to try
  • A story — or the beginning of one
  • A memory worth putting into words

What to Expect

The workshop is designed to be welcoming and low-pressure. There is no grading, no performance, and no requirement to share your writing with the group.

Sessions alternate between short presentations by the featured authors, group discussion, and guided writing time. The afternoon is meant to feel like a creative conversation — not a classroom.
